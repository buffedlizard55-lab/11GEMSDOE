WHY 0.1563 KEPT COMING BACK

Two different answers, both measured.

Full review →

For GEMSDOE1 and 5GEMSDOE: the same file

The published primary artifacts are byte-identical — SHA-256 7f00890a…, 570,890 bytes, identical pixels on the same grid. That is the whole explanation for that pair, and it is not a coincidence.

For everything else: a proxy that flatters us

8GEMSDOE shows the same 0.1563 from a different raster, and unrelated competitors sit at 0.1563 too. Every one of our files was tuned against the mapped catalogue — which the organisers confirmed is excluded from scoring. We were optimising a shortcut the contest is built to punish.

Re-scored on a leakage-free holdout — faults in blocks the model never saw, with a 1.2 km collar and the official mask applied. A model that only reproduces the training catalogue scores 0.0000 on it, so the split carries no shortcut. Our historical files score 0.18–0.45 there and still only 0.1563 on the leaderboard, because they were fitted on the very traces being held out. That gap is the finding.

H1 · REJECTED

Strain structure-tensor coherence

0.149795 against a control of 0.149986 on the 2026-09-27 paired blocks. Within noise, and it lost.

H2/H3 · +0.004

Fabric coherence and basement step

Nine derived channels from the magnetic, gravity, basement and conductivity bands. Wins 9 of 10 area targets; paired t = 1.3 on 4 folds. Real, not significant.

H4 · +0.005

Trace inpainting

Stop teaching “no fault” at the unmapped continuation of a mapped trace — which the organiser confirmed is in scope. Wins 8 of 10; indistinguishable from H2/H3.

And the one that is not a model change at all.

Emission geometry moved the holdout from 0.0943 at 40% emitted area to 0.1324 at 6% — a 40% relative gain from a shaping parameter, an order of magnitude larger than every model variant we tried. The metric charges every pixel of area at α=0.2 and forgives 300 m of positional error; those two facts set the optimum. See the sweep.

TWO BUGS WE CAUGHT IN OUR OWN CODE

Both would have produced a beautiful lie.

Emitting the answer key

The first emission sweep added the full label raster to the prediction. Inside a scored block, that is the held-out truth, so every arm scored a perfect 1.0000 and the trace-inpainting arm appeared to win by +0.19. In the contest the known faults are masked and the unknown ones are the target, so the honest analogue is to emit only the known catalogue.

Charging the whole map

The fast scorer did not zero predictions outside the scored region, so three quarters of every prediction was billed as false positive. Every number read about ten times too low. Both fixes have regression tests, and the leakage gate that caught the first one is now a hard abort in the experiment runner.

LIMITS WE WILL NOT PAPER OVER

What this file is not.

It is not a discovery

Every flagged pixel is a candidate structure. An expert has to look at it before it is a fault, and the competition's own prize process exists to make that judgement. Nothing on this site claims a discovery.

It is not a proven leaderboard gain

0.1366 is a holdout score on held-out mapped faults. The private set is faults an expert judged to be missing from the map, which may be subtler. Four folds cannot separate arms 0.004 apart.

Publishing it is a trade-off

This is an open competition. A public download hands the same artefact to every other entrant. We publish it because reproducibility and a working pipeline are the point of this repository, and we name the trade-off rather than hide it.

The data lineage has a weak link

The official tab needs a login. The bytes came from a checksum-pinned team mirror whose hashes match — that is verified, but it is not a fresh pull from DrivenData. Repairing this is the first task of the next session.
